# Gies AI for Impact Build-A-Thon — Impact Report

**Spring 2026 · Gies College of Business · University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign**

*Prepared for college leadership, project champions, mentors, judges, the keynote partner, the Office of the Provost, and prospective future sponsors.*

---

## Executive Summary

Over 24 hours on April 23–24, 2026, more than **120 Gies students** worked in **30 teams** to build production-grade agentic AI systems against five real business problems contributed by Gies operating leaders. **22 teams** — comprising **72 students** — completed the event end-to-end. Every team built on **Microsoft Copilot Studio**, and the event drove the **largest single-day usage of Copilot Studio recorded at UIUC**.

The Build-A-Thon was not a demo competition. Each track was anchored by a real Gies stakeholder ("project champion") who contributed an active operational problem, sample data, and a scoring rubric students were graded against. Output was concrete: working multi-agent systems with executable workflows, leadership briefings, and dashboards built in less than 24 hours.

Beyond the build itself, the event has generated tangible downstream impact — including a commitment from the **UIUC Office of the CIO** to bring builders onto its AI Solutions Development team for the summer and into next semester — and significant external visibility through organic LinkedIn coverage by students, mentors, judges, and the keynote partner Decasonic.

## Challenge Design — Real Problems, Real Champions

Each track was sponsored by a project champion drawn from Gies operating leadership and partner offices. The champion contributed: (a) a written challenge brief, (b) sample data drawn from real workflows, and (c) a published scoring rubric students were graded against.

| Track | Challenge | Champion |
|---|---|---|
| **Business Technology** | Prospect Identification — read job postings backwards to surface consulting opportunities for the Gies Business Advisory program | [**Charlie Farhoodi**](https://www.linkedin.com/in/charliefarhoodi/) |
| **Finance & Accounting** | Innovation Portfolio Budget Intelligence — help leaders decide which projects to stop, scale, or redeploy capital across | [**Adam King**](https://www.linkedin.com/in/king-adam/) |
| **General Innovation** | Career Navigator — turn a student's profile into personalized career recommendations, skill gaps, and a tailored job-search roadmap | [**Skylar Zhang**](https://www.linkedin.com/in/skylar-zhang/) |
| **Human Resources** | Candidate Evaluation System — multi-agent hiring evaluation with rubric-based scoring and audit trail | **[Julia Shubina Sabin](https://www.linkedin.com/in/juliasabin/)** |
| **Marketing & Sales** | Multi-Campaign Manager — unify marketing and sales workflows, evaluate campaign performance, draft stakeholder reports | [**Lindsey Savoie Halfar**](https://www.linkedin.com/in/savoie/) |

The challenges were curated, not improvised. The Business Technology track alone had **five drafted briefs** that were winnowed to one final challenge — a curation process repeated in every track to ensure each problem was scoped to fit a 24-hour build while still being operationally meaningful.

> **From the published Prospect Identification rubric:**
> *"The goal is a 0–100 overall fit score per posting, with a clear rationale and a ranked list at the end. Two reviewers (human or AI) using this rubric on the same posting should produce scores within ±6 points of each other. Every component above must be reproducible from the posting alone. If the agent cannot explain a score from the posting text, that score should not be used. Do not use company name prestige or personal networks as inputs to scoring."*

This was the bar across all five tracks — auditable, fair, and grounded in real operational decision-making.

## Operational Excellence

A high-touch support infrastructure made the 24-hour intensity sustainable.

- **Preparatory workshops** drew **200 signups** in the weeks leading up to the event, removing the "where do I even start?" friction so teams could begin building immediately at kickoff.
- **Discord** served as the primary support channel during the event. **50+ tickets** were opened for mentor and technical assistance.
- **HackClaw**, an AI helpdesk built specifically for the event, fielded **100+ student messages** on Discord and sent **500+ emails** during the 24-hour window — answering common questions, routing requests to mentors, and learning from resolved tickets in real time. HackClaw was itself an [AgentLab project](https://agentlab.illinihunt.org/projects.html), demonstrating how AI agents can be designed around real moments of work.
- **Five project champions**, supported by **seven mentors** drawn from Gies and partner offices, were instrumental in keeping every team focused and unblocked.

The challenge produced an unusually strong adoption signal for **Microsoft Copilot Studio**:

- The depth of work (multi-agent orchestration, Power Automate flows, Power BI dashboards, Outlook drafting, Excel logging) demonstrated that mainstream business students — without coding backgrounds — can build production-quality AI workflows when scoped well.

## Looking Forward

The Spring 2026 Build-A-Thon proved a model: real problems contributed by real operating leaders, scoped tightly, supported with strong infrastructure, and judged with rigor. Three threads to build on:

1. **More champions, more challenges.** The five tracks barely scratched the surface of operational problems within Gies and partner offices. Future iterations can add tracks (e.g., Operations, Risk, Student Success) with new champions, and can extend invitations to other UIUC colleges.
2. **A persistent pipeline.** Top builders are being absorbed by the Office of the CIO's AI Solutions Development team; this pipeline can be formalized so every Build-A-Thon graduates 5–10 students directly into university-wide AI work.
3. **External sponsorship.** The combination of named-champion problems, a working platform partnership (Microsoft Copilot Studio), keynote-level industry presence (Decasonic), and demonstrated student execution makes this an attractive sponsorship venue. Future sponsors can be named for individual tracks, prizes, or the keynote slot.

The Build-A-Thon is what experiential AI education looks like when business and technology are not separate conversations.
